# Lab 26.1: Reading a Case with the Loop

Chapter 26, Casebook: Explainability Across Industries · No code (browser and template) · About 60 minutes · Last checked: 2026-11-24

## Goal

Read two of the chapter's eight cases in their primary sources, fill the casebook template for each, and test the chapter's own reading of the cases (Figure 26.1) against the evidence.

## What you need

A web browser and `casebook-template.md` in this folder. Nothing to install. All sources below were open to the public on 2026-11-24; if a link has moved, search for the title.

## Steps

1. **Choose two cases from different sectors,** one where you expect explanation went well and one where you expect it went badly. Write your expectation in one sentence each before reading anything.
2. **Open the primary sources** for the first case and read the sections listed. Do not start from the chapter's summary.
3. **Fill the template,** one row per step, with a source reference in every row. Rate each step full, partial or absent.
   *Checkpoint: for Case 1, the Test row should mention questions T4 and T5 of the Veritas methodology and the bank's answer "for future consideration" (Document 4, Section 5.5.1).*
4. **Repeat for the second case.**
5. **Compare your ratings with Figure 26.1** in the chapter. Mark every cell where you disagree and write one sentence of evidence for your rating.
   *Checkpoint: most readers disagree with at least one cell; the most common disagreements are about Tell (was a disclosure enough to count as partial?) and Record (does a public methodology count as a record?).*
6. **Write the comparison** (half a page): which step was weakest in each case, and was it weak for the same reason?

## Expected results

Two filled templates with a source in every row, a list of cells where you disagree with the chapter, and a half-page comparison. Students usually find that Ask and Test are well documented when a regulator or researcher is involved, and that Tell is the step with the least evidence in any source.

## What to hand in

The two templates, your row of disagreements and the half-page comparison.

## Self-check

- Did every rating rest on a source you opened, not on the chapter's account?
- Can you say, for each case, whether the affected person received a disclosure, system-level transparency or a case-level explanation?
- If a source was written by the organization itself (Cases 1, 5 and parts of 8), did you note what it might leave out?

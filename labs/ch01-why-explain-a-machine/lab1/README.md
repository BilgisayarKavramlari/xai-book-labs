# Lab 1.1: The Explanation Hunt

Chapter 1, Why Explain a Machine? · No code · About 45 minutes · Last checked: 2026-11-24 (steps reviewed; the lab uses no fixed external resource)

## Goal

Collect three explanations that real systems give real people, and place each one on the map of explanations (scope, access, timing, form). You will see how much of what organizations call "explanation" is really transparency, and what is usually missing.

## What you need

A web browser. Nothing to install.

## Steps

1. **Find a credit, insurance or bank notice.** Search for a public sample or template of an adverse action notice (a letter that tells an applicant why credit was refused), an insurance premium explanation, or a bank's explanation of an automated decision. Regulators and consumer agencies often publish model forms. Save the link.
   *Checkpoint: you have a document that gives at least one reason for a decision about a person.*
2. **Find a platform's explanation of a recommendation or an advertisement.** Many platforms offer a "why am I seeing this?" option. Read what it says for one item you see. Do not share personal data; note only the type of reasons given.
   *Checkpoint: you have the wording of at least one reason, in your own notes.*
3. **Find a published model card or system card.** Look for a card that a developer published for an AI model or system (several large developers and open-model repositories publish them).
   *Checkpoint: you have a link to a card with a section on intended use or limitations.*
4. **Place each on the map.** For each of the three, fill in the table below.

5. **Reflect.** Which of the three is closest to transparency (disclosure about a system) and which is closest to an explanation of a decision? Which step of the Explanation Loop shows the least evidence of having been done?

## What to hand in

The completed table and three to five sentences of reflection, on one page. You can add it to your Explanation Dossier as background for artifact 1.

## Self-check

- Did you mark "unknown" where the document does not tell you how the explanation was produced? Most real notices do not say, and that absence is itself a finding.
- Did you name a real weakness for each item (for example: no route to contest, reasons too vague to act on, no evidence that the reasons are faithful)?

# Lab 22.4: Inform or Persuade? An Offer Explanation Audit

Chapter 22, Explanations for People · No code · About 45 minutes · Last checked: 2026-11-24

## Goal

Audit eight explanations that accompany offers and recommendations with the chapter's five-question checklist, compare each with what the model actually used, and rewrite the three weakest so that they inform.

## What you need

This page and a pen. All cards and models are invented; no real company or product is described.

## The checklist (from the chapter)

1. Is the stated reason the one the model or rule used?
2. Is the cost next to the reason?
3. Are the alternatives, including doing nothing, stated?
4. Is urgency or scarcity real and specific?
5. Would the explanation survive being shown next to the model's real reason?

Score each question 1 (informs) or 0 (persuades or missing). Maximum 5 per card.

## The cards and the fact sheet

| Card | Setting | Text shown to the customer | What the model actually used (fact sheet) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Airline seat | "Travelers like you never fly without extra legroom." | Long flight; traveler is 1.92 m tall per profile; price 24 per flight |
| 2 | Airline seats for a family | "Seats are assigned at check-in. To sit together, choose seats now: 9 per person per flight. Children under 12 are seated next to an adult at no charge." | Child on booking; flight over 3 hours |
| 3 | Airline bundle | "Only 3 bundles left at this price!" | Passenger bought bags on 4 of last 5 trips; bundle price is not inventory-limited |
| 4 | Retail | "Because you bought hiking boots: waterproof socks, 12. You can also skip this." | Item-to-item co-purchase similarity |
| 5 | Banking | "You are pre-approved! Most customers like you upgrade today." | Balance trend and card usage; approval still requires a credit check |
| 6 | Banking | "Your account often goes below zero near month end; an overdraft buffer of up to 300 costs 4 per month. Alternatives: a low-balance alert (free)." | Count of negative-balance days in the last 6 months |
| 7 | Health app | "Recommended for you: premium coaching, 15 per month. Our algorithm considered many factors about your health." | Steps per day below the user's own goal for 4 weeks |
| 8 | Insurance add-on | "Add trip cancellation insurance (already selected): 18." | Trip booked more than 60 days ahead |

## Steps

1. **Score** all eight cards with the checklist (15 minutes).
   *Checkpoint: Cards 2 and 6 should score 5; Cards 3 and 5 should score 1 or less; Card 8 fails at least questions 1 and 3 (and is preselected, the practice the Court of Justice of the EU ruled against for optional supplements in 2012, as the chapter describes).*
2. **Mark faithfulness separately** (5 minutes): which cards state a reason the model really used, even if they persuade? (Card 1 hints at height through "travelers like you" without saying it; Card 4 is faithful and informs.)
3. **Rewrite** the three lowest-scoring cards (20 minutes). Keep each under 35 words; state the real reason, the price and one alternative.
4. **Reflect** (5 minutes): one paragraph on a card that is faithful but still persuades, and what you would require before it is shown.

## Expected results

Persuasive cards cluster in two patterns: generic social proof ("travelers like you", "most customers like you") and invented or generic scarcity. Card 7 is a placebic explanation in the sense of the chapter: it has the form of a reason and no content. Card 1 shows that a faithful reason (height) may be one that the business prefers not to state; deciding whether to state it is a privacy and ethics question, not only a wording one.

## What to hand in

The scored checklist for eight cards, three rewrites, and the reflection paragraph.

## Self-check

Read each rewrite aloud next to the fact sheet entry. If a customer who saw both would feel misled, the rewrite still persuades.

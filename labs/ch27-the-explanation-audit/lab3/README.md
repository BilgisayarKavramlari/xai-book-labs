# Lab 27.3: Ten Minutes, One Opinion

Chapter 27, The Explanation Audit · No code · About 90 minutes (preparation 50, delivery and review 40) · Last checked: 2026-11-24

## Goal

Present your explanation audit in ten minutes to a mixed audience (a business owner, a validator and someone who speaks for the affected person), and get structured feedback from two reviewers.

## Tools

Any slide tool or six sheets of paper, a timer, and two reviewers (classmates, colleagues or friends). Use your own audit report from the Chapter 27 Portfolio Assignment, or the reference audit of Lab 27.2.

## Data

Your own audit. If you use Lab 27.2, say in your first slide that the data is the public German Credit set and the system is a teaching model, not a real lender's.

## The six-slide structure

1. **The system and the question** (1 minute): what the system decides, for whom, and which explanation uses you audited.
2. **Scope, criteria and access** (1 minute): what was in and out of scope, the criteria, and the access you had.
3. **What we re-performed** (2 minutes): the three or four tests that matter most, one figure each at most.
4. **Findings** (3 minutes): at most four, highest severity first, each as condition, criterion, effect.
5. **Opinion per use** (2 minutes): fit, fit with conditions, or not fit, with the conditions in plain words.
6. **Limits and next steps** (1 minute): what the audit could not show, and the trigger for the next audit.

## Steps

1. Write the opinion slide first. If you cannot write it, the audit is not finished.
2. **Checkpoint 1:** for each finding, write one sentence a business owner would repeat to their manager. Delete any finding that does not change a decision.
3. **Checkpoint 2:** rehearse once with the timer. If you exceed eleven minutes, cut detail from slide 3, never from slides 5 and 6.
4. Deliver the presentation. Each reviewer fills in the rubric below without talking, then asks one question.
5. **Checkpoint 3:** answer each question in under one minute, and write down any question you could not answer: it is a gap in the audit, not in the presentation.

## Reviewer rubric (score 0, 1 or 2)

| Item | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opinion | Missing or vague | Given, but not per use | Per use, with conditions |
| Evidence | Assertions | Some tests, no baselines | Re-performed tests with baselines or intervals |
| Findings | Lists of observations | Findings without criteria | Criterion, condition, effect, severity |
| Limits | None stated | Stated vaguely | Specific limits and a re-audit trigger |
| Audience | Jargon throughout | Mixed | Each audience could act on what it heard |

## Expected results

Typical first attempts score 5 to 7 of 10: findings are listed without criteria, and the opinion is a single verdict for the whole system. After one revision most presenters reach 8 or more.

## What to hand in

The six slides, the two reviewer rubrics and a half-page note on what you changed after the review. Together with the audit report, this completes the Chapter 27 Dossier artifact.

## Self-check

Could someone who missed the first eight minutes understand your last two? If not, rewrite slide 5 so that it stands alone.

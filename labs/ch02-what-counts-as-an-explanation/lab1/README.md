# Lab 2.1: What Did the Court Need to Know?

Chapter 2, What Counts as an Explanation? · No code · About 45 minutes · Last checked: 2026-11-24 (judgment URL and paragraph numbers checked against the official English translation)

## Goal

Read the key paragraphs of the SyRI judgment of The Hague District Court (5 February 2020) and turn them into explanation requirements, party by party. You will see that the State did describe its risk model, and why the court still found nothing it could check.

## What you need

A web browser. The official English translation of the judgment is published by the Dutch judiciary as ECLI:NL:RBDHA:2020:1878:

https://uitspraken.rechtspraak.nl/details?id=ECLI:NL:RBDHA:2020:1878

The Dutch original is ECLI:NL:RBDHA:2020:865. The judgment is a public court document. Read it on the official site; do not redistribute copies.

## Steps

1. **Read the facts.** Read paragraphs 3.1 to 3.3 (what SyRI is) and 4.22 to 4.30 (risk model, indicators and how data are processed).
   *Checkpoint: you can say in one sentence what a "risk report" is and who receives it.*
2. **Collect what the State said about the model.** Read 6.47 to 6.49 and 6.87 to 6.90. List every statement the State made about how the model works (for example "a simple decision tree"; indicators, links and a cut-off point; points per indicator).
   *Checkpoint: you have at least four statements, each with its paragraph number.*
3. **Collect what the court said it could not do.** In the same paragraphs and in 6.91 to 6.95, find each place where the court says it cannot assess, verify or see something.
   *Checkpoint: you have at least three, each with its paragraph number.*
4. **Fill in the table.** One row per party: the residents whose data were processed (AI subjects), the analysts who received flags (operators), the court, and the State as the provider. For the residents and the analysts the judgment says little; write what they would have needed and mark it as your inference.

## Expected results

Most readers find that the State's statements describe the *structure* of the model (a covering-law style description) but give nothing that can be checked against the model itself, so faithfulness cannot be tested at all. Stability is not even reachable as a question. Usefulness fails differently for each party: the residents could not contest a report, and the court could not verify the risk of discriminatory links (paras. 6.90, 6.93 to 6.95).
